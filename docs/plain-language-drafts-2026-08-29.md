# Plain-language rewrites — DRAFT for client approval

**Status: not live, not wired into the site.** Nothing here is on the website. Site
copy is locked verbatim to approved source documents by `scripts/verify-copy.mjs`,
so none of this can ship until the client approves it and it is added to an
approved source document (the same route we used for
`docs/imrs-prime-swissbionic.txt`).

## Why

The site's audience is largely 65+. Patient-facing health material has a
long-standing readability standard of **grade 6–8** (AMA / NIH). The current copy
measures **grade 11.6 overall**, and the worst pages sit at college level.

| Page | Now | Draft |
|---|---|---|
| Mental Health | 13.9 | see below |
| Holistic Health | 13.1 | see below |
| Sleep Health | 12.6 | see below |
| Energy | 12.3 | see below |
| PEMF | 11.9 | see below |

Readability scores are a smoke alarm, not a certificate — the CDC explicitly
rejects them as a sufficient measure. The structural rules matter more: sentences
under 20 words, paragraphs under 3 lines, active voice, main point first, and
technical terms explained where they appear.

## Ground rules used in these drafts

1. **Claim strength is preserved exactly.** Where the current copy says PEMF *is
   effective*, the draft still says it helps — not more, not less. Plain language
   is a readability change, not a claims change. Claims worth a separate review
   are listed at the end.
2. **No new benefits introduced.** Every idea in a draft traces to the current copy.
3. Technical words are explained in place, not sent to a glossary.
4. Headings and structure are unchanged, so the drafts drop into the existing
   page components without layout work.

## Claims the client may want to review separately

These are **not** readability problems, and the drafts above leave every one of them
exactly as strong as it is today. They are flagged only because the site also
carries the notice that the PEMF system is not a medical device and is not
intended to diagnose, cure, mitigate, prevent or treat any disease. A page that
states a definite result sits in tension with that notice, and that is a judgement
for the client and their counsel, not for us.

| Page | Current wording | Nature of the claim |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep Health | "PEMF is an effective tool for improving sleep quality." | States effectiveness as fact. |
| Mental Health | "PEMF is safe, effective, non-invasive and non-addictive tool." | States safety and effectiveness as fact. |
| Energy | "…and you will feel the difference." | Promises a specific outcome to every reader. |
| Mental Health | "…improve mental acuity and learning ability…" | A cognitive-improvement claim. |
| Products | "Biofeedback for wellbeing purposes sensor… SpO2 and HRV evaluation." | Measuring blood oxygen reads as a medical function to many readers. |

A softer construction ("many people find…", "may help you…") would reduce that
tension, but it changes what the client approved, so it is deliberately **not**
done in the drafts above. The hedged variant is in the next section, offered as a
separate choice.

## Hedged variant — a second, separate decision

These replace the plain-language drafts **only where a claim is involved**. Every
other rewrite above is unaffected. Take this variant whole, take individual lines,
or take none of it.

**What hedging changes:** it moves each line from asserting a result as fact to
describing what the product is and what people report. It does not weaken the
writing — it relocates the confidence from the outcome to the offer.

**The trade-off, stated plainly:** hedged copy converts less confidently than a
promise. "You will feel the difference" is a stronger sales line than "see how it
feels for you." That is a real business cost, and it is the client's call whether
the reduced exposure is worth it. This is not legal advice; a lawyer should make
the final judgement.

| | Wording |
|---|---|
| Now | PEMF is an effective tool for improving sleep quality. PEMF is a non-invasive, non-addictive, drug-free approach for promoting better sleep. |

### Products — the Exagon Sense sensor

**This one is different in kind, and should not be paraphrased.**

"Bifunctional photoplethysmografy sensor… SpO2 and HRV evaluation" is the
manufacturer's own product description, approved verbatim from the Swiss Bionic
page. Rewriting it risks describing the product inaccurately, which is a worse
problem than the one being solved. Blood-oxygen measurement reads as a medical
function to most people, so there are three honest options:

1. **Keep it verbatim and attribute it** — present the specification as the
   manufacturer's, not the practice's, wording.
2. **Omit the SpO2 and HRV line** and keep the rest of the description. The
   sensor's role on the page is that it adjusts intensity during a session, which
   the remaining copy already says.
3. **Keep it as is.** It is accurate to the product.

Recommended: option 2 on the practice's own page, since the measurement detail
earns little for a wellness audience and carries the most medical reading.
This is a decision for the client, not a rewrite to be applied quietly.
